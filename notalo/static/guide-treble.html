Treble clef notes

The treble clef (𝄞, also called the G clef) is the top staff in piano music and the only staff for most singers, flute, violin, guitar and trumpet parts. Here is every note on it, labeled.

Lines and spaces

Bottom → topMnemonic
LinesE G B D FEvery Good Boy Does Fine
SpacesF A C EFACE

The clef's curl wraps around the second line from the bottom — that line is G, which is why it is called the G clef. Every other note follows from there, one letter per step.

Ledger lines

Notes above or below the staff sit on short extra lines called ledger lines. Below the staff: D (just under the bottom line), then middle C on the first ledger line, B, A and so on. Above the staff: G (just over the top line), A on the first ledger line, B, C…

How to read it fast

  1. Anchor on three notes you know cold: bottom line E, middle line B, top line F.
  2. From the nearest anchor, count steps: each line-to-space or space-to-line step is the next letter (E F G A B C D E…).
  3. Watch for a sharp or flat after the clef — the key signature — which changes that letter in every octave.

Frequently asked questions

What are the lines of the treble clef?

From the bottom line up: E, G, B, D, F — Every Good Boy Does Fine.

What are the spaces of the treble clef?

From the bottom space up: F, A, C, E — they spell FACE.

Where is middle C on the treble clef?

On a short ledger line just below the staff. The note in the first space above it is D, then E on the bottom line.
